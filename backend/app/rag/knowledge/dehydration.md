# Dehydration

## Overview
Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluids and electrolytes than it takes in, leaving it without enough water to carry out normal functions.

## Common Causes
- Fever, which increases fluid loss through sweating and faster breathing.
- Vomiting or diarrhea.
- Excessive sweating from hot weather or vigorous exercise.
- Not drinking enough water during illness or busy daily activities.

## Common Signs
- Thirst and dry mouth or dry skin.
- Dark-colored urine or urinating less frequently than usual.
- Fatigue, dizziness, or lightheadedness.
- In infants and young children: crying without tears, sunken eyes, or fewer wet diapers.

## General Hydration Guidance
Mild dehydration is commonly managed by sipping water, clear broths, or oral rehydration solutions steadily rather than all at once.

## When to Seek Emergency Care
Seek immediate medical attention for signs of severe dehydration, including extreme thirst, confusion, fainting, rapid heartbeat, inability to keep fluids down, or very little to no urination.
