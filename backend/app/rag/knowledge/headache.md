# Headache

## Overview
Headaches are a very common type of pain in the head or upper neck. Most headaches are not caused by a serious medical condition, though some can signal an underlying issue that needs medical attention.

## Common Types
- **Tension headaches**: The most common type, often felt as a dull, aching band of tightness around the forehead, temples, or back of the head and neck. They are frequently linked to stress, muscle tension, fatigue, or eyestrain.
- **Migraines**: Typically cause throbbing or pulsing pain, often on one side of the head, and may be accompanied by nausea, vomiting, or sensitivity to light and sound.
- **Sinus headaches**: Associated with pressure or fullness around the cheeks, brow, or forehead, often alongside nasal congestion.
- **Cluster headaches**: Less common, causing severe piercing pain around one eye or one side of the head.

## General Self-Care and Comfort Measures
For mild, occasional headaches, general comfort measures include resting in a quiet, dark room, staying hydrated, applying a cool or warm compress to the forehead or neck, and managing stress or sleep schedules.

## When to Seek Immediate Medical Care
Seek emergency medical care if a headache:
- Comes on suddenly and severely (like a thunderclap).
- Occurs after a head injury.
- Is accompanied by fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizures, weakness, numbness, slurred speech, or vision changes.
- Worsens steadily over days or weeks or is new after age 50.
