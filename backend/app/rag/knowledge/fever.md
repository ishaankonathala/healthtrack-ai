# Fever

## Overview
A fever is a temporary rise in body temperature above the normal range (around 98.6 F or 37 C). It is typically a sign that the body's immune system is responding to an infection, such as a viral or bacterial illness.

## Common Causes
- Viral infections, such as the common cold or influenza.
- Bacterial infections, such as strep throat, urinary tract infections, or pneumonia.
- Heat exhaustion, certain inflammatory conditions, or reactions to vaccinations.

## General Care and Monitoring
- Rest and avoid strenuous activity while your body recovers.
- Drink plenty of fluids, such as water, clear broths, or oral rehydration solutions, to prevent dehydration.
- Wear lightweight clothing and keep the room at a comfortable temperature.
- Monitor temperature and accompanying symptoms over time.

## When to Seek Medical Attention
Contact a healthcare professional or seek urgent care if a fever:
- Reaches 103 F (39.4 C) or higher in an adult, or occurs in an infant under 3 months old.
- Lasts more than a few days or keeps returning.
- Is accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, rash, or seizures.
